## 1. Recording Screen Visual Language

### 1.1 Status text instead of animated mic

Replace the animated mic indicator from the original brief's §2.1. The recording state is communicated through clear text:

- **"Ready to record"** — shown before the user taps Record.
- **"Recording"** — shown while actively capturing.
- **"Paused"** — shown when paused (the timer stops, waveform visualization dims).

Text uses the `display_md` type style from the home screen tokens.

**Design principle:** no decorative animation. Visual motion must represent real state — a beating heart of actual recording, not a performance of recording. The waveform below conveys what's happening; nothing else needs to.

### 1.2 Live waveform visualization

Replace the "simple waveform or bars" spec in the original brief's §2.1 with a multicolor level meter that provides live feedback on audio levels.

**Dimensions (target values; tune during device testing):**
- Height: approximately 48–72 device-independent pixels (roughly 1/2 inch on typical devices). The goal is "prominent and readable, not cramped" — final value determined during device testing.
- Width: spans the full width of the recording screen minus standard horizontal padding (20px each side per design tokens).

**Rendering approach:**
- Dense sample points producing a smooth, continuous waveform feel — aim for visual smoothness over strict sample count.
- Waveform scrolls right-to-left as audio is captured, with newest samples entering from the right.
- When paused or not recording, the waveform is flat (a centered horizontal line in `text_muted`).

**Color states (firm rule — these states and meanings do not change):**

Sample color is determined by the instantaneous audio level:

- **White** (`text_primary`): sustained low levels. Indicates "too quiet — user may not be heard."
- **Kraken accent** (`accent`): levels in the healthy range. The target state.
- **Red** (`danger`): peaks indicating clipping risk. Indicates "too loud — back off the mic."

The three-state color meaning is a product requirement and must ship exactly this way. The specific thresholds that trigger each color state are **target values to be tuned during device testing**:

- Starting thresholds for tuning: approximately -30 dBFS (transition from white to accent) and approximately -3 dBFS (transition from accent to red).
- Attack and release timing should smooth the color transitions so individual samples don't flicker rapidly between colors. Suggested starting point: ~10ms attack, ~100ms release. Adjust during testing.
- Calibration goal: normal speaking voice at typical meeting distance produces primarily accent color. Quiet speech or distant speakers produce white. Loud speech or phone-too-close produces red peaks.

**Calibration validation during device testing:**

Record in realistic conditions (normal conference room voice, quiet huddle room, louder open office) and verify:
- Normal speech produces mostly accent color consistently.
- Speaking too quietly or far from the phone produces white.
- Loud speech or the phone placed too close produces red peaks.

If the waveform reads as constantly red or constantly white during normal meetings, the thresholds need adjustment. The final numbers are whatever produces accurate feedback on typical devices, not the starting values above.

### 1.3 Playback waveform (v1.1 nice-to-have)

Not required for v1, but flag for future: when playing back retained audio, show the same waveform style with a position indicator scrubbing across it. Don't build for v1, but don't design the recording waveform in a way that precludes it later.

## 2. Recording Time Limit Warnings

The original brief specified warnings at 18:00 and 19:30. Expand to include a 5-minute check-in:

- **At 15:00:** A non-blocking inline notice: "5 minutes remaining on free-tier recording." Small, positioned below the timer, not a modal. Includes a subtle "Upgrade" link but doesn't interrupt.
- **At 18:00:** Slightly more prominent inline notice: "2 minutes remaining."
- **At 19:30:** Banner across the top of the recording screen: "30 seconds remaining. Tap Stop to save, or upgrade to continue."
- **At 19:45:** Modal: "Upgrade to continue or stop recording now. Whatever's captured will be saved either way." Two buttons: "Upgrade" and "Stop recording."
- **At 20:00:** Recording stops cleanly. User lands on the review screen with audio, transcript-in-progress, and all data intact. A dismissible banner at the top of the review screen: "Recording stopped at the 20-minute free limit. You can review what was captured."

**Principle:** never take away what the user already has. Time limit enforcement is about future recording, not losing past recording.

## 4. User-Initiated Summary Generation

The original brief's §4.3 described auto-starting summary generation when transcription completes. Change this.

Summary generation is **user-initiated**. When transcription completes, the user lands on the transcript view with:

- Full transcript visible and editable.
- A "Generate summary" button prominently placed (below the transcript header or floating at the bottom of the screen).
- A brief sub-label: "Review the transcript, then summarize when you're ready."

This gives users a chance to correct transcription errors before feeding them to the summarizer, producing better summaries and fewer "garbage in, garbage out" disappointments.

After first summary generation, the "Regenerate" and "Refine" actions behave as originally specified.

### 4.1 Specific flow state impacts (for agent clarity)

This change replaces or modifies several assumptions in the original brief that the agent must update:

- **State machine states:** the original brief implicitly had a "generating summary" state that auto-entered after transcription. Replace with "transcript ready, awaiting user action" as the completion state after transcription. "Generating summary" is now entered only by explicit user tap on "Generate summary."
- **Completion UX:** the original brief described a flow where users landed on a screen showing summary-in-progress. Replace with a flow where users land on the transcript, review/edit freely, and tap a button to generate the summary.
- **Button logic:** the "Generate summary" button must be present, visible, and functional at all times after transcription completes. If a summary has already been generated, the button becomes "Regenerate" (per §4.4 of original).
- **Test flows:** any integration test that assumes summary generation automatically follows transcription must be updated to include the explicit user tap on "Generate summary."
- **Exit criteria:** original brief's criteria around summary timing should be updated to reflect user-initiated flow, not timing from transcription end.

The agent should audit the original brief's references to summary generation and update anywhere the auto-start assumption is embedded.

## 6. Audio Retention — Expanded

### 6.1 Retention policy confirmed

- **Delete audio after transcription:** audio file deleted immediately once transcription completes.
- **90-day retention (default):** audio kept until 90 days past last access. Auto-deleted on the daily retention sweep if past due.
- **Keep until I delete:** audio kept indefinitely, user manages manually.

### 6.2 "Last access" definition

For the 90-day clock, "access" means audio-specific interaction: playback, scrubbing, or export of the audio file. Viewing the recording's summary, transcript, or detail screen without touching audio does **not** reset the clock.

Rationale: users who review summaries routinely but never replay audio would otherwise accumulate audio indefinitely. The clock should track actual audio usage.

### 6.3 Total audio storage cap

In addition to per-recording retention, Meeting Notes enforces a total audio storage ceiling. Default: **2 GB across all retained audio for this spoke.**

**This is an intentional product rule.** The silent-during-recording behavior is deliberately chosen, not an oversight. If the agent or a future reviewer believes warnings should be added, notifications should be surfaced, or "keep until I delete" should override the cap, **raise for human review — do not modify the spec.** This behavior was considered and chosen; softening it would introduce exactly the ambient notification noise the product philosophy is designed to avoid.

**Behavior when the cap is reached:** silent auto-deletion of oldest audio files. No interruption to active recording. No surprise prompts. The app quietly manages storage in the background.

**Specifically:**

- Recording is **never interrupted** due to storage cap being reached. If a new recording or extension of an existing recording would push total audio over the cap, older audio files are deleted automatically to make room.
- Deletion order: oldest by last-access date first (consistent with §6.2's definition of "last access" — playback, scrubbing, or export).
- Deletion cascades: if the oldest file doesn't free enough space, the next-oldest is also deleted, and so on until there's room for the new audio. This may mean deleting multiple old recordings' audio in one sweep during a long recording session.
- Transcripts and summaries of affected recordings are **always preserved**. Only the audio blob is deleted. The transcript, summary, entity references, folder assignment, and all other metadata stay intact.
- The affected recording entity remains visible in the user's Meeting Notes list, searchable and readable. It simply no longer has playback or audio export available.

**No pre-cap warnings.** No 80%/95% warnings. Users should not be nagged about storage management; the app handles it.

**No notifications during recording.** If the cap is hit mid-recording and old files are purged to make room, no banner, modal, sound, or indicator appears. Recording continues uninterrupted.

**Where the user can discover what happened (if they look):**

- In the per-recording detail view of an affected recording: the retention microcopy reads "Audio was deleted to free up space." (See updated §6.6 below.) This replaces the usual "retained until [date]" line for that recording only. It is not a notification — it's honest state information for users who open that recording's detail.
- In Meeting Notes settings, the storage display reads something like "1.92 GB of 2 GB used. Oldest audio is auto-deleted to stay within the limit. [Configure]." The configure link opens the cap settings.
- In the audit log (for users who check): a `audio_auto_deleted` event per affected recording, with timestamp and reason ("storage_cap_enforcement").

**The cap is configurable** in Meeting Notes' spoke-level settings. Users can raise or lower it. Lowering the cap below current usage triggers immediate auto-deletion of the oldest audio to fit; the same silent behavior applies.

**"Keep until I delete" retention does not override the cap.** A user who marks every recording as "keep until I delete" will still have their oldest audio auto-purged when the cap is reached. This is the right tradeoff: the cap exists to prevent unbounded bloat, and respecting individual retention preferences over storage hygiene would defeat its purpose.

### 6.4 Settings explanation (must be prominent and clear)

Because silent auto-delete is aggressive behavior, the settings explanation must be unmistakably clear. In Meeting Notes' spoke-level settings near the storage cap configuration, show:

> **Storage Limit**
>
> Kraken keeps your audio files under this size. When you record new audio and the limit would be exceeded, Kraken automatically deletes the oldest audio files to make room. This happens silently during recording — no interruptions.
>
> **Transcripts and summaries are always kept**, even when their audio is auto-deleted.
>
> This applies to all recordings, including those marked "Keep until I delete." The limit exists to prevent your device storage from filling up unexpectedly.
>
> Current usage: [X] GB of [Y] GB · [Configure]

This language is deliberate. Users who read it will understand the rule. Users who don't read it will experience "the app just worked" without ever hitting a frustrating interruption. Users who later wonder "where did my old audio go" will find a clear answer when they look.

### 6.5 UI for storage state

In Meeting Notes settings, a clear display:
- Current storage used (e.g., "1.2 GB of 2 GB used").
- List of recordings sorted by storage size, with "Export and delete audio" and "Delete audio" quick actions.
- The cap as a settable value.

### 6.6 Microcopy update for retention states

Per-recording retention microcopy, aligned with actual state:

- Delete-after-transcription: "Audio was deleted after transcription. Transcript and summary are kept."
- 90-day with audio present: "Audio retained until [date]."
- Keep-until-delete: "Audio retained until you delete it."
- Audio deleted per 90-day retention: "Audio was deleted on [date]."
- Audio deleted by user action: "Audio was deleted on [date]."
- **Audio deleted by storage cap:** "Audio was deleted to free up space."

The storage-cap-deletion microcopy is deliberately low-key. It's accurate but not apologetic. No "sorry," no "[date]" prefix, no invitation to adjust settings — just honest state. Users who want to understand more can check settings; the per-recording detail stays quiet.

The 90-day-deletion and user-deletion microcopy both include the date; the storage-cap-deletion does not. Rationale: the date of storage-cap deletion is often less meaningful to the user (it happened in the background as a side effect of recording something else), so highlighting it would invite confusion. The date is still available in the audit log for users who need it.

## 7. Delete Recording Mid-Transcription

User attempts to delete a recording while transcription is actively running:

1. First confirmation: "Cancel transcription? This will stop transcribing and let you delete the audio." Two buttons: "Cancel transcription" and "Keep transcribing."
2. On confirm: transcription job is cleanly cancelled (not just UI dismissed — actual job cancelled, buffers released, partial work discarded).
3. Second confirmation: "Delete audio and all associated data for this recording? This cannot be undone." Two buttons: "Delete" and "Keep."
4. On confirm: all data for this recording is destroyed.

Separating these two confirmations respects that they're two different decisions. A user who cancels transcription might still want to keep the raw audio for later.

## 8. Force-Quit Recovery

### 8.1 Incremental audio flush

Recording must write audio to disk incrementally during capture, not buffer the entire recording in memory until Stop. Flush cadence target: every 3-5 seconds of audio. This ensures the on-disk file is never more than a few seconds behind what was actually captured.

The audio file format should support appending (m4a supports this via its container structure, but implementation details matter — confirm with the audio service team that incremental writes are genuinely robust).

### 8.2 Session marker

When a recording starts, write a "session in progress" marker to the vault with:
- Recording ID
- Start time
- Output file path
- Last-flushed timestamp

Update the marker on each flush. Clear the marker when the recording stops normally (via Stop button).

### 8.3 Recovery on relaunch

On app launch, Meeting Notes checks for any unclosed session markers. If found:

Show a recovery dialog (after the user unlocks the vault, before the normal home screen):

**"You were recording earlier."**

Body: "Kraken captured about [X] minutes of audio before the app was closed. Would you like to keep it?"

Buttons:
- **"Keep and transcribe"** — treat the captured audio as a completed recording, proceed to transcription.
- **"Delete"** — with one-step confirmation, discard the captured audio.

The user never loses captured audio without an explicit choice. Silent data loss on force-quit is not acceptable.

### 8.4 Edge cases

- App killed by OS under memory pressure: same recovery flow on relaunch.
- Phone crashes or reboots: same recovery flow on relaunch.
- Incomplete recording is very short (under 10 seconds): still offer recovery. Users will force-quit occasionally by accident; respect even the tiny captures.
- Incomplete recording is corrupted: if the audio file on disk cannot be played or transcribed, show the user: "Your earlier recording couldn't be recovered. Kraken has discarded the unrecoverable file." Log the incident for diagnostic purposes; do not silently delete.

## 9. Multi-Language Support

### 9.0 Scope and acceptance criteria

Multi-language support has two distinct layers, each with its own v1 scope:

**Layer 1 — Transcription (all seven languages ship in v1, unconditionally):**

All seven target languages (English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Chinese, Japanese, Korean) ship with working transcription in v1. Faster-Whisper handles these languages well and language detection is automatic. There is no "held for v1.1" path for transcription; it either works (which it will) or the language is removed entirely.

**Layer 2 — Summary generation (conditional per language):**

Summary generation quality varies by language because it depends on Gemma 4's output quality for structured prompts in that language. Each language has a clear v1 acceptance test:

1. **Test:** Produce summaries for 5+ real-world meeting recordings in that language. Have a native speaker review each summary.
2. **Pass criterion:** The native speaker rates the summary as useful and would send it to a colleague without significant editing. At least 4 of 5 test summaries must meet this bar.
3. **Fail criterion:** Summaries contain hallucinations, break structure (missing sections, wrong formatting), produce unnatural phrasing a native speaker would reject, or otherwise embarrass the product.

**What happens per language based on test results:**

- **English:** Must pass. English is the reference language; if English summaries fail, the whole feature has a problem that is not a language issue. Non-shippable failure — fix before ship.
- **Spanish, French, Portuguese, Chinese, Japanese, Korean:** If any of these fail the acceptance test, **that specific language's summary generation is held for v1.1**. Transcription still ships for that language; only summary generation is gated. The language picker shows the language as supported, and the recording/transcript flow works, but attempting to generate a summary in that language shows a clear message: "Summary generation for [language] is coming soon. Your transcript is ready."

**No middle ground.** The bright line is: either a language's summaries pass the acceptance test and ship fully, or its summaries fail and are held for v1.1 while transcription still ships. There is no "ship with a prompt tweak on a known-weak language" path. If Spanish summaries are rough, either fix them before ship (the preferred outcome — Spanish should be reachable) or hold Spanish summary generation for v1.1 while Spanish transcription continues to work.

The goal is to ship a product where every advertised capability works well. Half-working features damage trust more than acknowledged limits do.

### 9.1 Supported languages for v1

Transcription and summary generation supported in:

- English (default)
- Spanish
- French
- Portuguese
- Chinese
- Japanese
- Korean

All processing remains on-device. No cloud translation, no cloud transcription, ever.

### 9.2 Language detection

**Auto-detect by default.** Whisper handles language detection at the start of transcription with good accuracy. The detected language drives both transcription and subsequent summary generation.

### 9.3 Language display

After transcription, show the detected language subtly beneath the transcript header:

> **Transcribed: Japanese (detected)**

Style: small caption, `text_muted`. Tappable — opens a language override picker.

### 9.4 Manual language override

User can override the detected language in two places:

- Per-recording: tap the detected-language chip, pick from the supported list. Triggers re-transcription with the new language.
- Default language for new recordings: in Meeting Notes' spoke settings, a language picker. Default is "Auto-detect."

### 9.5 Summary language matches transcript language

v1 constraint: **summary is produced in the same language as the transcript.** A Japanese transcript produces a Japanese summary; a French transcript produces a French summary.

Cross-language translation (transcribe in Japanese, summarize in English) is a v2 feature. Do not build it in v1.

### 9.6 Quality expectations

Whisper's transcription quality and Gemma 4's summarization quality vary by language. Expected strongest: English, Spanish, French, Portuguese. Expected more variable: Chinese, Japanese, Korean.

Actual shipped scope depends on §9.0 acceptance testing. The settings copy must reflect reality, not aspiration:

- If all languages pass: "Kraken supports transcription and summarization in English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Chinese, Japanese, and Korean. Quality is strongest in English."
- If some languages' summaries were held for v1.1: "Kraken transcribes in English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Chinese, Japanese, and Korean. Summary generation is available in [list of passing languages]. Additional languages coming in a future update."
- If transcription works for all but summaries only ship for English: "Kraken transcribes in [all 7 languages]. Summary generation is currently English-only; additional languages coming in a future update."

Pick the copy that matches what actually shipped. Do not advertise support that was held back.

### 9.7 Summary prompt engineering per language

The default summary prompt (§4.2 in original brief) is designed for English output. Extending to other languages requires either:

- A single prompt that works in all languages (simpler but lower quality).
- Per-language prompts tuned for each language's conventions (higher quality, more maintenance).

**Recommendation for v1:** ship with a single well-tested prompt. Apply the acceptance test from §9.0 to each language. Any language that fails has summary generation held for v1.1 per §9.0 — do not ship with obviously weak summaries and a promise to fix them later. Per-language prompt refinement is a v1.1 effort, done for languages that were held back, to bring them up to shipping quality.

This keeps v1 honest: every shipped summary capability meets the acceptance bar; anything that doesn't is deferred rather than released with excuses.

### 9.8 UI localization (separate from content localization)

Content localization (transcripts and summaries in the user's language) is in v1. UI localization (button labels, menu items, system copy translated to the user's language) is a separate future mission. v1 ships with English-only UI regardless of content language.

This is a defensible tradeoff but needs to be honest about it: a French user can record and transcribe in French, but the app's menus remain in English for v1.

If UI localization becomes a priority, it's a dedicated mission covering every user-facing string. Out of scope here.

## 11. Audio Encoding Specification

Audio capture must use specific encoding parameters. These are not platform defaults — the recording service must explicitly configure these values.

### 11.1 Specification

- **Container format:** m4a (MP4 audio)
- **Codec:** AAC-LC (AAC Low Complexity profile)
- **Bitrate:** 64 kbps, constant bitrate
- **Channels:** mono (1 channel)
- **Sample rate:** 16 kHz

### 11.1.1 Note on bit depth

Bit depth is a property of PCM (uncompressed) audio capture, not of AAC output. The platform's audio input path typically captures at 16-bit PCM by default before encoding to AAC; this is fine and does not need explicit configuration in most cases. Do not attempt to configure "bit depth" on the AAC encoder itself — that parameter doesn't exist meaningfully for AAC.

If the platform exposes an input bit depth setting during capture, 16-bit is appropriate. If it doesn't, use the default and proceed. The audible and transcription quality of 16 kHz-sample AAC output at 64 kbps is identical whether the source capture is 16-bit or 24-bit PCM — the AAC encoding step discards the extra precision anyway.

### 11.2 Rationale

These parameters are chosen for Meeting Notes' specific needs, not borrowed from platform defaults:

- **16 kHz sample rate** is optimal for Whisper transcription. Whisper downsamples higher rates to 16 kHz internally, so recording at higher rates wastes storage with no transcription benefit.
- **Mono** is sufficient for speech. Stereo doubles file size without improving transcription quality.
- **64 kbps AAC** is well above the threshold where voice quality degrades (around 32 kbps) and well below the bitrates intended for music fidelity (128+ kbps). Industry standard for voice-focused recording.
- **Constant bitrate** (rather than variable) produces predictable file sizes, which makes the storage cap math work reliably.

### 11.3 Resulting file sizes

At these settings:

- ~0.48 MB per minute of audio
- ~29 MB per hour of audio
- 20-minute free-tier recording: ~10 MB
- 2 GB storage cap holds ~70 hours of audio

### 11.4 Platform implementation

**iOS (AVAudioRecorder):**

```
AVFormatIDKey: kAudioFormatMPEG4AAC
AVEncoderAudioQualityKey: AVAudioQualityMedium
AVEncoderBitRateKey: 64000
AVNumberOfChannelsKey: 1
AVSampleRateKey: 16000.0
```

**Android (MediaRecorder):**

```
OutputFormat: MPEG_4
AudioEncoder: AAC
AudioEncodingBitRate: 64000
AudioChannels: 1
AudioSamplingRate: 16000
```

### 11.5 Device testing requirement

Some devices' audio hardware prefers specific sample rates and will resample internally if asked for something else. This can produce artifacts at the resampling boundary.

During device testing, record a sample on each target device (minimum: iPhone SE, iPhone 15 Pro, Pixel 8, plus one mid-tier Android device) and verify:

- The output file is actually 16 kHz (inspect file metadata).
- The audio sounds clean on playback (no warbling, no digital artifacts).
- Whisper transcribes it cleanly.

If any target device produces poor 16 kHz output, fall back to 48 kHz mono 64 kbps (same bitrate, higher sample rate) on that device specifically. Larger files but cleaner source audio. Document which devices use the fallback path. Transcription pipeline handles the downsampling regardless.

### 11.6 Not configurable by users in v1

The audio encoding is not exposed as a user setting in v1. These parameters are chosen deliberately and exposing them would add UI surface without meaningful user benefit — users who want higher-quality recordings generally want them for music or professional audio work, which isn't Meeting Notes' use case.

If real demand emerges for alternative recording quality (e.g., a user wanting higher bitrate for archival purposes), add it as a v1.1 setting. For v1, the spec above is fixed.

## 12. Background Recording and Persistent Indicators

Recording must continue reliably when the screen locks, the screen sleeps, or the user switches to another app. This is table-stakes functionality for a voice recording tool; without it, users lose data in routine scenarios.

### 12.0 Priority Tiers (Read First)

This section contains substantial engineering surface — platform-specific background modes, notification systems, interruption handling, session markers, recovery flows, safety caps, and manufacturer-specific edge cases. **Do not attempt all of this in a single implementation pass.** Ship in tiers:

**Must-have for v1 (block v1 release until these work):**
- Background recording continues when screen locks or app is backgrounded (§12.1).
- Persistent notification on both platforms showing recording state (§12.2 — basic form without Pause action).
- Stop action from the notification cleanly stops recording (§12.2).
- Force-quit recovery (§8) works for normal recordings and OS-killed sessions.
- Five-hour safety cap with warning ladder and clean stop (§12.4).
- iOS OS-provided red pill status indicator works (nothing to implement; verify it appears).

**Should-have for v1 (ship if time permits, otherwise fast-follow):**
- Phone call interruption handling — auto-pause on incoming call, auto-resume on call end (§12.3).
- 30-minute battery disclosure notification (§12.5).
- Tapping notification body opens the app to active recording screen (§12.2).

**Nice-to-have (v1.1 or later — do not delay v1 for these):**
- Pause action on notification (in addition to Stop) (§12.2).
- Device-specific handling for manufacturers with aggressive battery optimization (Samsung, Xiaomi, Huawei) (§12.6 item 9).
- Recovery dialog UI polish for very short partial recordings (§8 edge cases).

**If the must-have tier is unstable or incomplete, do not proceed to should-have or nice-to-have work.** Reliable core behavior beats feature breadth every time here. A v1 that has only must-haves but never loses audio and handles background reliably is far better than a v1 with all the fancy features but flakey on Android manufacturers that matter.

### 12.1 Platform requirements

**iOS:**

- Declare `audio` background mode in `Info.plist` (`UIBackgroundModes` includes `audio`).
- Configure `AVAudioSession` category as `.record` or `.playAndRecord` depending on other audio needs. Use `.record` for Meeting Notes — the app takes audio focus during recording, which will pause other audio apps (music, podcasts). This is correct behavior; recording a meeting while a podcast plays would capture garbage.
- Set audio session options carefully: do NOT use `.mixWithOthers` during recording. Do use `.duckOthers` if the app needs to play any audio confirmation tones.
- Activate the audio session before starting recording; deactivate on stop.
- The OS will show its standard microphone-in-use indicator (red pill in the status bar) automatically. This is platform-provided privacy disclosure; do not suppress it and do not try to replicate it.

**Android:**

- Create a foreground service with `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE` permission declared in `AndroidManifest.xml`.
- Service type: `microphone` (per Android 14+ requirements).
- Start the foreground service when recording begins; stop the service when recording ends.
- The foreground service requires a persistent notification. This notification is the recording indicator users will see when the app is backgrounded.

### 12.2 Persistent recording indicator

A visible indicator must be present whenever recording is active, regardless of app foreground/background state. This serves two purposes: privacy disclosure (users know the mic is live) and user control (users can stop from outside the app).

**iOS implementation:**

- The OS red-pill status bar indicator is automatic; nothing to implement.
- Additionally, post an interactive notification on the lock screen and in Notification Center. Content:
  - Title: "Kraken — Recording"
  - Body: "Started [relative time]. Tap to view."
  - Action buttons: "Stop" (stops recording cleanly, captured audio preserved) and optionally "Pause" (pauses, user can resume later from the app).
  - Notification is persistent; it does not auto-dismiss.

**Android implementation:**

- The foreground service notification is required and acts as the indicator. Configure it as:
  - Title: "Kraken — Recording"
  - Text: "Started [relative time]. Tap to view."
  - Icon: A small Kraken mark variant suitable for notification tray.
  - Priority: default (not high — should be persistent but not intrusive).
  - Action buttons: "Stop" (primary) and optionally "Pause."
  - Sticky/non-dismissible while recording. Cleared when recording stops.

**Both platforms:**

- Tapping the notification body opens the app directly to the active recording screen.
- The "Stop" action button stops recording cleanly: flush remaining audio to disk, finalize the file, transition recording state to stopped, return the user to the review screen next time they open the app. No confirmation dialog — tapping Stop from a notification is an explicit user action.
- The "Pause" action button (if included) pauses recording and updates the notification to reflect paused state. Resuming requires opening the app.
- The notification updates its "Started [N] minutes ago" text periodically (every minute or so) to reflect current elapsed time.

### 12.3 Interruption handling

Recording must handle audio interruptions gracefully:

**Phone calls:**

- Incoming call begins: pause recording automatically. Update notification and in-app UI to show "Paused for phone call."
- Call ends (declined, ignored, or completed): resume recording automatically within a second or two. Update UI to show "Recording" again.
- User answers the call: recording stays paused for the duration. Auto-resume when the call ends.

**Other audio interruptions (Siri, alarm, etc.):**

- Pause during the interruption, resume when the audio session is returned to the app.

**Do not lose captured audio in any interruption scenario.** Flush to disk before pause, append on resume.

### 12.4 Five-hour safety cap

A hard maximum recording length of **5 hours** applies to all recordings, regardless of entitlement tier. This is a safety rail against runaway recordings (forgotten recordings, device in bag all day, etc.) — not a pricing limit.

**Warning ladder (similar to the 20-minute free-tier ladder):**

- At 4:45:00: non-blocking inline notice on the recording screen and in the persistent notification: "15 minutes remaining."
- At 4:55:00: more prominent notice: "5 minutes remaining. Kraken will stop recording at 5 hours."
- At 4:59:00: modal if app is foregrounded, notification update if backgrounded: "Recording will stop in 1 minute."
- At 5:00:00: clean stop. Captured audio is preserved. User lands on review screen (or sees completion notification if backgrounded). Banner on the review screen: "Recording stopped at the 5-hour safety limit. You can start a new recording if needed."

**Why 5 hours specifically:**

- Long enough that legitimate long-form recording (workshops, all-day conferences, extended interviews) fits.
- Short enough that forgotten recordings don't consume meaningful storage or battery.
- Chosen as a safety mechanism, not a tier feature. Paid users hit this too.

**The 5-hour cap is not configurable by users in v1.** Exposing it as a setting invites edge-case bugs and opens the door to "Kraken ran my battery dead because I set the cap to 24 hours." If real user demand emerges for longer limits, address in a future version with appropriate safeguards.

### 12.5 Battery disclosure

Background recording consumes noticeable battery over long sessions. A 5-hour recording with screen off uses significantly more battery than normal idle. This is not catastrophic, but users deserve honest information.

Add a one-time informational note the first time a recording crosses 30 minutes:

> "Long recordings use more battery. You can keep Kraken in the background — recording will continue even when the screen is off. [Got it]"

Shows once per device install, dismissible with "Got it," not a repeated warning.

### 12.6 Testing requirements

Background recording is a common source of subtle bugs. Test these scenarios explicitly on real devices (simulators don't reliably reproduce background behavior):

1. **Lock screen during recording:** screen locks → recording continues → unlock reveals recording still active with correct elapsed time.
2. **App switch during recording:** user opens another app → notification is visible → recording continues → return to Kraken shows recording still active.
3. **Extended background:** record for 30+ minutes with app in background → open Kraken → recording still active, audio captured is complete and not truncated.
4. **Phone call during recording:** incoming call → recording auto-pauses → call ends or is declined → recording auto-resumes → resulting audio has a clean pause/resume boundary, no corruption.
5. **Notification Stop action:** tap Stop from lock-screen/notification → recording ends cleanly → app shows review screen on next open.
6. **Notification Pause action (if implemented):** tap Pause → recording pauses → open app → resume from UI → verified clean pause/resume in final audio.
7. **Five-hour cap:** deliberately run a long recording (can fast-forward time in test harness) → verify warnings at each threshold → verify clean stop at 5:00:00 with audio preserved.
8. **Battery-drained recording:** start recording with phone at 15% battery → let it run until critical battery warning appears → verify recording continues until OS force-closes the app → verify partial audio is recoverable via force-quit recovery flow (§8).
9. **Android aggressive battery management:** on devices from manufacturers known for aggressive battery optimization (Samsung, Xiaomi, Huawei), verify the foreground service is not killed during extended background recording.

### 12.7 Exit criteria additions

Add to §10's exit criteria list:

- Recording continues when screen is off, app is backgrounded, and through short interruptions.
- Platform-appropriate persistent indicator is shown during recording (iOS notification + red pill; Android foreground service notification).
- Notification Stop action cleanly stops recording from outside the app.
- Phone call interruptions pause and auto-resume correctly.
- Five-hour safety cap enforces cleanly with appropriate warnings.
- Battery-drain scenarios preserve captured audio via force-quit recovery.
